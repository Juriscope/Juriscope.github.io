Beginner's guide

Everything you need to write your first research essay.

  1. Pick a question

    Choose a topic you care about or a question that makes you curious:

    • Do you love animals? Write about the legality of animal testing. Should policies regarding this be changed? Is it ethical?
    • Do you care about the environment? Write about the implications of littering. Should consequences be more extreme? To what extent should we make an effort to protect the environment?

    Simple topics you care about can narrow down into a question. Even if what you are passionate about seems silly, go for it! The last thing you want to do is write about something you do not care about.

  2. Research your question

    Once you have a topic, begin gathering information from credible sources. Look for academic articles, court decisions, government publications, legal journals, and reputable research organizations.

    As you research:

    • Keep track of your sources
    • Take notes on important findings
    • Compare different perspectives
    • Look for evidence that supports or challenges your initial ideas

    Remember that research is about understanding an issue, not just collecting information that agrees with you.

  3. Develop your argument

    A strong research paper does more than summarize existing information. It presents a central argument, explores different perspectives, and uses evidence to support its conclusions.

    Consider organizing your paper into:

    • Introduction: Introduce your topic and research question.
    • Background: Explain the relevant legal or historical context.
    • Analysis: Examine evidence, arguments, and different perspectives.
    • Discussion: Explain what your findings mean.
    • Conclusion: Summarize your argument and its broader implications.

  4. Write and revise

    Your first draft does not have to be perfect. Focus on getting your ideas on the page before worrying about every sentence.

    After completing your draft, revise it for clarity, organization, grammar and strength of argument. Make sure your claims are supported by evidence and that your sources are properly cited.

  5. Submit your work to Juriscope

    Once your paper is complete, review our submission guidelines and submit your work through our submission form.
